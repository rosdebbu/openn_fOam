## 🌍 Part 1: How Nature is Computed (The OpenFOAM Core Physics Blueprint)

To simulate anything in nature—whether it is air over a wing, hot smoke billowing into the sky, water splashing against a wall, or weather systems—physics relies on **4 Universal Conservation Laws**.

```
                ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                │             THE 4 CONSERVATION LAWS OF NATURE            │
                └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                             │
      ┌──────────────────────┬───────────────┴──────────────┬──────────────────────┐
      ▼                      ▼                              ▼                      ▼
┌──────────────┐      ┌──────────────┐               ┌──────────────┐       ┌──────────────┐
│ 1. MASS      │      │ 2. MOMENTUM  │               │ 3. ENERGY    │       │ 4. WATER     │
│ (Volume/ρ)   │      │ (Press/Grav) │               │ (Temp/Buoy)  │       │ (VOF Phase α)│
└──────────────┘      └──────────────┘               └──────────────┘       └──────────────┘
```

### 1. Conservation of Mass (Volume & Continuity)
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{U} = 0 \quad \text{(Incompressible)} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{U}) = 0 \quad \text{(Compressible)}$$
- **What it means in nature**: Matter cannot vanish or appear out of nowhere. If fluid enters a control volume $V$, the exact same amount must leave unless the density $\rho$ changes.

### 2. Conservation of Momentum (Navier-Stokes with Gravity & Pressure)
$$\frac{\partial (\rho \mathbf{U})}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{U} \otimes \mathbf{U}) = \underbrace{-\nabla p}_{\text{Pressure Force}} + \underbrace{\mu \nabla^2 \mathbf{U}}_{\text{Viscous Shear}} + \underbrace{\rho \mathbf{g}}_{\text{Gravity}}$$
- **Pressure Gradient ($-\nabla p$)**: Fluids are pushed from high pressure regions to low pressure regions.
- **Gravity ($\rho \mathbf{g}$)**: The universal downward body force ($g = -9.81\text{ m/s}^2$ on Earth, $-1.62\text{ m/s}^2$ on the Moon, $0$ in space). Denser fluid gets pulled harder!

### 3. Conservation of Energy (Temperature $T$ & Buoyancy)
$$\frac{\partial (\rho C_p T)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho C_p \mathbf{U} T) = \nabla \cdot (k \nabla T) + S_h$$
- **Boussinesq Buoyancy Approximation**:
  $$\rho(T) = \rho_0 [1 - \beta (T - T_0)] \implies \mathbf{F}_{\text{buoyancy}} = -\rho_0 \beta (T - T_0) \mathbf{g}$$
- **What it means in nature**: Heating a fluid expands its volume and decreases its density ($\rho$). Because gravity pulls the surrounding cold (heavier) fluid downward, the hot (lighter) fluid is forced **upward against gravity**. This creates thermal plumes, chimney draft, and atmospheric weather!

### 4. Multiphase & Water (Volume of Fluid - VOF)
$$\frac{\partial \alpha}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\mathbf{U} \alpha) = 0$$
$$\rho_{\text{mix}} = \alpha \rho_{\text{water}} + (1 - \alpha) \rho_{\text{air}}, \quad \mu_{\text{mix}} = \alpha \mu_{\text{water}} + (1 - \alpha) \mu_{\text{air}}$$
- **What it means in nature**: To simulate water, rain, sloshing, or ocean waves, OpenFOAM's `interFoam` solver tracks the water fraction $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ across the cells, computing surface tension ($\sigma$) and hydrostatic pressure ($p = \rho g h$).
